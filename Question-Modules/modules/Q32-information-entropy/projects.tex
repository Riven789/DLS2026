\moduletitle{Q32 --- Deeper projects}{Quantifying information flow with entropy}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.7em]
\item \textbf{Entropy-triggered abstention.} Build a selective-prediction wrapper
around a small instruction model on a trivia or multiple-choice set. Use predictive
entropy (and the logit margin) as the abstention score; plot the
accuracy--coverage (risk--coverage) curve and compare entropy against a verbalised
``how sure are you?'' baseline. \emph{Deliverable:} the curve + a short note on
where entropy fails as a confidence proxy. (Links to Q10--Q12.)

\item \textbf{Reproduce the binning artefact, then kill it.} Take a 2--3 layer MLP
on a toy classification task. Reproduce a ``compression-phase'' information-plane
plot with \texttt{tanh} + coarse binning, then show it weaken/vanish with (a)
\texttt{ReLU}, (b) a finer/adaptive binning, and (c) a KSG estimator.
\emph{Deliverable:} three plots and a paragraph on what, if anything, survives.

\item \textbf{Attention-entropy atlas.} For a small open transformer, compute
per-head attention entropy across layers on a fixed prompt set; cluster heads by
entropy profile and cross-reference against known head taxonomies (induction heads,
attention sinks, positional heads). \emph{Deliverable:} a heatmap + a claim about
which clusters are real vs.\ which are averaging artefacts, tested by ablation.

\item \textbf{Estimate vs.\ intervene bake-off.} Pick one specific information-flow
hypothesis (e.g.\ ``the subject token's information reaches the final position
through layer $\ell$''). Answer it two ways --- a variational MI bound (InfoNCE)
\emph{and} activation patching --- and write up where they agree, where they
diverge, and which you would trust in a paper.

\item \textbf{LLM-as-compressor.} Reimplement the Del\'etang et al.\ experiment at
toy scale: use a frozen small LM as the probability model inside an arithmetic
coder and measure bits/token on held-out text; compare against \texttt{gzip} and
against the LM's reported perplexity. \emph{Deliverable:} a table showing
compression ratio $=$ perplexity in different units.
\end{enumerate}
