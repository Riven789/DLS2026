\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item If an LLM's claim to be conscious is caused by its training data, is there \emph{any} verbal behavior that would count as evidence of experience? Specify it or argue none exists.
\item IIT implies a purely feed-forward network has $\Phi=0$ no matter how it behaves, while a simple recurrent circuit can have $\Phi>0$. Is this a strength or a fatal flaw of the theory? Defend a view.
\item The ``gaming'' problem says any named behavioral sign can be faked by a capable system. Does this doom all empirical tests, or only behavioral ones? What survives?
\item Suppose two reputable theories disagree on whether a given system is conscious. How should a policymaker act under that uncertainty---and does the moral cost of being wrong change the answer?
\item Distinguish access consciousness from phenomenal consciousness with a concrete AI example where a system clearly has one but arguably not the other.
\end{enumerate}
